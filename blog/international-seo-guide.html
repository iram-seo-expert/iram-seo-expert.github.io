International SEO Guide: How to Get Customers in Any Country

If your business wants customers in other countries, ranking in your home market is only the start. International SEO makes sure the right version of your site appears to the right people in each country and language.

1. Decide which markets to enter first

Start with the markets where you already get enquiries or have a logistical advantage. In Search Console and GA4, filter by country to see where interest already exists, then check search demand in each target market before you invest in content.

2. Choose a URL structure

  • Country domains (such as example.ro) give the strongest local signal, but each domain builds authority separately and costs more to maintain.
  • Subfolders (such as example.com/ro/) are usually the simplest option because they share the authority of one domain.
  • Subdomains (such as ro.example.com) work, but are treated more like separate sites.

3. Use hreflang correctly

Hreflang tells Google which language or regional version of a page to show. Use language and region codes such as en-GB or ro-RO. Every version should link to all the others and to itself, and it helps to add an x-default for users who match no version. Errors here are common, so test it in a crawler.

4. Localise, do not just translate

Search behaviour differs by country. Even the same language differs between countries (UK, US and Australian English use different words), and searchers in other languages phrase queries differently again. Use native speakers for content, and adapt currency, units, delivery details, contact information and examples.

5. Do local keyword research

Do not translate your keywords word for word. Research what people in each market actually type, check local competitors, and map each keyword to a page that matches the search intent.

6. Avoid automatic redirects by location

Forcing users and crawlers to a version based on IP address can stop Google from seeing all your versions. Offer a visible language or country switcher instead.

7. Build local authority

Links and mentions from relevant sites in the target country help. Look at local directories, industry bodies, partners and press. If you have a physical presence there, set up a local Google Business Profile.

8. Check the technical basics

Use a CDN or fast hosting so pages load quickly abroad, keep Core Web Vitals healthy, and make sure your sitemap lists every language version.

9. Check local rules

Privacy, cookie and consumer rules differ between countries and regions such as the UK, EU and US. Confirm the requirements for each market with a qualified adviser before launch.

10. Measure by market

Track impressions, clicks and conversions per country in Search Console and GA4, and expect a few months before a new market shows steady results.
