How to Get Cited in Google AI Overviews and ChatGPT: A Practical GEO Guide

Generative Engine Optimization (GEO) is the practice of making your content easy for AI search tools to find, understand and cite. Nobody can guarantee a citation, but these steps improve your chances.

Start with normal SEO

AI features in search draw on pages that are already indexed and allowed to show a snippet. If Google cannot crawl or index a page, it will not be used. Check Search Console > Pages first.

1. Answer the question directly

Put a clear, short answer right under the heading, then add detail. A 40 to 60 word direct answer is easier for a system to quote than a long introduction.

2. Write in questions and answers

Use headings that match real questions, such as "What is technical SEO?" or "How long does SEO take?". This mirrors how people ask AI tools.

3. Make your entity clear

  • Use the same name, description and contact details everywhere.
  • Have a clear About page that says who you are and what you do.
  • Link your website to your LinkedIn and other professional profiles.

4. Add structured data

Article, Organization and FAQ schema help machines understand a page. Structured data does not guarantee rich results or AI citations, but it removes ambiguity. Make sure the markup matches what is visible on the page.

5. Earn mentions from trusted sites

AI systems and search engines both lean on signals of trust. Quality backlinks, guest articles and consistent mentions on relevant sites help.

6. Do not block crawlers by accident

Review your robots.txt, meta robots tags and any firewall settings. Keep pages fast, mobile friendly and accessible without login.

7. Keep content accurate and current

Update pages when facts change and show the date. Clear, correct and specific content is more useful to both readers and AI tools.

How to measure it

Track impressions and clicks in Search Console, and test important questions in AI tools from time to time. Results vary by query and change often, so treat it as a trend, not an exact score.
